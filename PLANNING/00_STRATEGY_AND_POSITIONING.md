# Portfolio Strategy & Positioning

## Final Positioning (Reviewer Decision)

**Role:** Frontend Developer who builds thoughtful, well-crafted websites for businesses and digital products.

**Not:**
- A student or learner
- An agency
- A full-stack expert
- A designer competing with award-winning creative studios
- An "AI" or "vibe coder"

**Central message:**
> Thoughtful websites. Carefully built.
> I build responsive web experiences that bring visual design, useful functionality, and clear communication together.

**Stack to show:** React · Next.js

**Why this direction:** It connects what you already do (business websites in Next.js) with what you want next (client work). It communicates care and capability without claiming mastery you haven't established. It is honest and conversion-oriented.

---

## Current vs Recommended Emphasis

| Current emphasis | Recommended emphasis |
|---|---|
| What I'm learning | What I've built |
| A list of skills | Evidence of those skills in projects |
| A timeline of progress | How I approach and execute work |
| Project cards with lots of text | Prominent previews and concise project stories |
| A generic invitation to connect | A clear invitation to discuss a website project |

**Rule:** Educational background and technical learning stay, but they move to supporting positions (About or footer). They never define the identity.

---

## Reviewer Decisions (Hard Calls)

1. **No branch created.** Repository left untouched per your instruction.
2. **No AI mention anywhere.** Not in copy, not in tech stack, not in project descriptions. Removes "vibe coder" risk entirely.
3. **Single-page scroll structure.** Few projects = one page is enough. Anchor sections: Hero → Work → Why Me → About → Contact. Reduces friction; keeps visitors in the funnel.
4. **Navigation:** Work, About, Contact only. No "Blog," no "Learning," no "Resume" in primary nav. Learning can live in About.
5. **Skills:** Not a badge list. Skills appear inside project stories (e.g., "Built with Next.js and Tailwind") and in a compact "Tools" line in About.
6. **Contact:** Primary CTA in hero. Sticky bottom CTA on mobile. Contact section at bottom + footer email. No multi-step form.
7. **Animation:** Functional only. Smooth scroll, subtle hover states. No award-site parallax, no reveal-on-scroll sequences, no auto-playing media.
8. **Tone:** Direct, confident, humble. No "ninja," "guru," "passionate about technology," or generic agency fluff.
9. **Visual identity:** Editorial digital-craft. Typography-first, warm neutral canvas, strong project imagery, one restrained accent color for CTAs.

---

## Visitor Journey

1. **Understand you** — Hero answers who you are, what you build, and what opportunities you're open to. Direct path to work from the first screen.
2. **Experience your work** — Three carefully selected projects with a purposeful scrolling screenshot gallery. Interaction reveals more of each site instead of asking visitors to imagine it.
3. **Understand your contribution** — Short project summaries: purpose, your role, key implementation decisions, links to live site or source. Deeper details available for interested visitors.
4. **Decide whether to contact you** — Short explanation of how you work, honest statement about projects you take on, clear contact action. Contact reachable from navigation without completing the journey.

## Confidence & Offer

Do both: show work and invite contact. Hero leads with "Explore selected work" and secondary "Discuss a project." Concise availability statement clarifies openness to suitable website projects and frontend opportunities. No expert claims, no learner announcements. AI is a tool, not a public identity — transparent if asked directly.

## Motion

Purposeful only. Scrolling screenshot gallery is worth exploring because it displays actual websites. No long preloader, no constant movement that delays access to work or contact.
